# Changelog

Notable changes to this project are recorded here. The project has no tagged releases; dated entries name the commit that introduced them.

## [Unreleased]

### Realism

Editor tools under **Aurelle > Realism** rebuilt the look of the showroom, the cars, the people and the cyclists' bicycles. The run order is in the README.

#### Rendering & lighting

- PC (quality level Ultra, now also the Editor's default) renders in HDR with Forward+ and SSAO. A global post-processing volume adds ACES tonemapping, colour adjustments, white balance, bloom and a light vignette, and the desktop camera adds SMAA and dithering.
- PC shadows are soft (high quality), 4096 px, in four cascades over 90 m (previously 50 m). Additional-light shadows are enabled, and reflection probes blend and use box projection.
- Quest (Low) keeps its pipeline: no HDR, no post-processing and no SSAO.
- The showroom has baked lighting: Baked Indirect, GPU lightmapper and baked ambient occlusion.
  - The six recessed spots, 21 new ceiling downlights (each with a visible lens) and seven wall washers, now mounted at the ceiling, are baked.
  - The hero car's key and rim spots are Mixed, and the key casts a real-time soft shadow on PC.
  - The fill light is off. The showroom's real-time lights drop from 13 to 2.
- Reflection probes are baked: a box-projected probe for the hall, one for each car on display (the hero car and the three premium cars) and one for the glazing. About 1,500 light probes cover the showroom, with a coarse grid over the district.
- The floor has soft contact shadows under the premium cars.
- Sky ambient light (0.62 → 1.0) and sky reflections (0.55 → 0.8) are stronger, so shaded facades stay bright without the fill light. Street lamps are dimmed for daylight (30 → 12).
- On Android, lightmaps import at up to 1024 px and reflection cubemaps at 128 px.

#### Hero car studio

- The render setup builds `Aurelle Hero Studio` under `Showroom Lighting`:
  - A softbox canopy over the podium: four flush ceiling light boxes in a 2.1 × 4.8 m frame, baked emissive, so the paint shows long, clean highlight lines.
  - A contact shadow: a quad on the podium deck with a texture that the tool rasterises from the car's meshes (tyre contact, underbody and footprint layers). It renders at every quality level, fades out while the explorer's view is exploded or an assembly is out, and fades back once the car is whole.
  - A planar reflection of the car in the podium deck on PC (Ultra, not in XR or in the X-ray view), through the new shader `Aurelle/Planar Reflective Lit`, a mirror renderer (`AurelleMirrorRenderer`) and layer 30 (`Aurelle Planar Mirror`).
- The hero reflection probe is 512 px (256 px on Android) and sees the canopy overhead. The key and rim spots are lower, so the clear coat's and the deck's highlights stay out of the clipped range.
- Grid downlights inside the canopy's plan are skipped.

### Car Explorer

The explorer of `65e4de9` (seven stages) became, first, three views with one take-out per assembly and then a structure explorer that takes the car apart level by level.

- **Structure tree.** The car is a tree of systems, assemblies, sub-assemblies and parts: 13 systems, 54 assemblies (15 of CarConcept's own, the V6 and 38 internals assemblies) and 1,653 nodes in SampleScene, at most six levels deep. Nodes tagged with the new `CarPartInfo` component (kind, id, English and Uzbek names and descriptions, system, material, explode hints, fine detail) define the tree under them; CarConcept's own glTF nodes are grouped by name rules.
- **Views.** Normal: the complete car. X-ray: the body shell fades in 0.6 s into light-blue glass with a bright rim (shader `Aurelle/X-Ray Ghost` in `Assets/Resources`) and a soft spot light fades in over the car. Exploded: every assembly comes out over 1.5 s, CarConcept's own in three workshop-manual tiers (floor tub with the wheels and the V6, seats and cockpit, body frame with the roof, windscreen, doors and sections on top) and the internals outward on a height tier per system, on the podium. The layouts keep clear of the floor, the ceiling, the other display cars, the furniture and the viewer.
- **Drill-down.** A click on a part takes its assembly out of the car in one piece (0.8 s); the next click makes it come apart one level, and every further click opens the next level along the clicked part's chain, down to the single part. A click on another assembly's part puts the first back and then takes that one out (0.6 s each); in the exploded view a click opens the assembly in place. Opening a deeper node from the tree opens every level above it in one move. R, `[` or Page Down closes the deepest level, then puts the assembly back, then steps the view back (exploded → X-ray → normal); a click on empty space only clears the selection. Assemblies inside the body switch the normal view to X-ray when they come out; the cabin floor and the underbody stay in place and only come apart.
- **The assembly comes toward the visitor, outside the car.** One placement rule serves every assembly (`FindVisitorPlace`): its place lies on the horizontal line from its rest centre to the visitor's eye, so on screen it comes nearer instead of sliding across the view, about 1.4–2.6 m from the eye (from 0.9 m in XR) at chest height where there is room.
  - Two rules are never broken: its box, in one piece and with every open level, ends outside the car's footprint (the car's rest bounds seen from above, grown by 0.15 m; over the roof or the bonnet counts as inside), and the car's box never stands between the eye and its centre.
  - Where the line has no room it first comes nearer (its box down to 0.5 m from the eye on the desktop, 0.35 m in XR, and at most 65 % of the view's height), then turns round the eye as little as it must (3° steps growing to 180°) to be in the free view (`HudSafeViewport`, `XrViewArea` in XR) and clear of Remy, the other cars, the furniture and the walls. A visitor pressed against the car gets the nearest place beside them outside the footprint, in view where possible.
  - On the desktop, when no place keeps every rule for the eye as it is (the visitor stands too close to the car), the visitor glides straight back from the car during the move, up to 1.5 m in 0.15 m steps and as far as the walking body can go, and the assembly is placed for the eye there; its path keeps clear of the visitor all along the glide, and a walking key or mouse look ends it (`CarExplorerStepBack`, provided by the walkthrough). Never in XR and never while inspecting.
  - In XR the distances are tried in 0.05 m steps, an open level comes 0.9 m from the eye keeping 0.35 m, and an opened assembly too big for the view so near backs toward the car, up to 1.5 m, until its levels are in view.
  - A place out of view, or in the way of something, also pays for filling more than 65 % of the view's height, so a wheel or a door no longer ends beside the eye bigger than the view.
  - Opening levels leaves the assembly where it is; a spread that would reach into the footprint (any part, or the whole box with its open levels) moves it toward the visitor as little as it must (`KeepOutOfFootprint`). Where that push keeps 0.5 m from the eye but leaves the levels partly out of view, it stands unless a new place shows more of them within 15° round the eye (or anywhere, when the push shows less than half of them); only where no push keeps 0.5 m from the eye does the whole assembly take a new place by the same rules.
  - What is out stays while the visitor walks; an open, a close or a click again brings it to them again when it is out of view or behind the car from where they stand (`RefitTakenOut`).
- **Paths instead of straight lines.** A travelling assembly lifts straight up until its bottom clears the car's top along its way by 0.12 m (a door or a wheel leaves sideways from its own place, when the whole sideways leg keeps 0.35 m from a visitor standing there), travels over the car and settles; a path that would pass within 0.35 m of the visitor below head height goes over their head. Every path is checked as it will run, at 240 points: where it touches another part after leaving its place it goes higher, and where any leg (rounded corners included) comes nearer the visitor's eye below their head than 0.35 m, it goes over their head. Put back runs the path in reverse. Corners are rounded, the speed is even along the path with smoothstep easing, and the car's top comes from a 0.1 m height map of its exact picking meshes, built once at start (`EnsureBodyHeights`, `BuildPath`).
- **Moves in stages.** Levels close, an assembly goes home, an assembly comes out, levels open, one after another (`SetMoveWindows`): a take-out takes 0.8 s, a put back with a level open 0.96 s, a switch to another assembly 0.6 s home and 0.6 s out. Doors, wheels and the seats keep their heading (turned, the seats' parts ran into the car); only the cockpit turns toward the viewer, unless a part hangs under another assembly's part.
- **X-ray with the internals.** The floor tub and the cabin trims (dash, binnacle, centre console, cage, pillars) turn to faint glass, so the suspension, the bulkhead systems, the cross-car beam, the seat frames and the steering column show; the seats, the steering wheel, the pedals, the wheels, the V6 and the internals stay solid. The fade starts from a stand-in lit like the paint (real-time lights, blended reflection probes, clear coat), so the body no longer darkens for a moment; windows cast no shadow while they fade; a missing glass shader logs one warning instead of one per renderer per frame.
- **Inner content.** The V6 and the internals are drawn only where they can be seen (X-ray, exploded, their own assembly or the front section out, and during moves into or out of such a state), never cast shadows, and have their selection boxes off while hidden.
- **Picking.** The car's parts, the V6 (through its merged meshes) and the internals are picked by their exact meshes, cooked on worker threads into a separate physics scene and ready on the first Play Mode frame: 1,302 parts (819 at Low). In the X-ray view a click passes through the glass to the part behind it. Walls, glazing, other cars and furniture in front block the selection, and clicks on the HUD never select what is behind them.
- **Selection.** The highlight also lowers metallic and takes more of the accent on dark colours, so it shows on dark metal and on the glTFast materials of the car and the engine. While anything is apart, the parts outside the deepest open level are darkened by 40 %. Parts show readable names (for example Hood instead of BodyHood) in English and Uzbek.
- **Rest pose.** After any view, take-out or drill-down, the car returns exactly to its authored pose and its own materials, also when the explorer is disabled or destroyed. A command given during a move continues from where the parts are.
- **XR grabs.** In the X-ray view the glass shell cannot be grabbed, and a part that is held or still gliding back when a move starts returns to its place instead of staying where it was dropped.
- **Model contract.** The explorer creates no UI. It implements `ICarExplorerModel` (`CarExplorerModel.cs`: the views, the tree, `Select`, `Open`, `CloseTo`, `PutBack`, `Back`, `ResetAll`, `Highlight`, `TryGetNodeBounds`, `Changed`) and registers in `CarExplorerModels`, which the HUD, Remy and the inspect camera read. The controller is split into `CarExplorerController.cs`, `CarExplorerStructure.cs` and `CarExplorerLayout.cs`.
- The console panels, the part card and WELCOME panel next to Remy, and Remy's nameplate of the earlier explorer are gone; the HUD replaces them.
- Selection colliders fit each part's mesh, and the bootstrap no longer logs an error in scenes without the car.

### Car Explorer HUD

- New CAD-style HUD (`CarExplorerHud*.cs`), created at runtime and the explorer's only UI: the STRUCTURE tab with a pooled tree and a breadcrumb top left, the view switch (Normal / X-ray / Explode), EN / UZ and `?` on top, the selected node's card on the right, and a bottom row with the key hint for the current state, an explore chip and an eye button.
- The tree opens the path to the selection, marks nodes that are apart and fits long names so siblings stay distinguishable; a click selects, a double click or the row's explode icon opens, hovering highlights.
- The card shows the kind and status (OUT OF THE CAR, APART), the name, "system · parent", the description (More / Less), the material and the units and parts the node holds, and the buttons Take out or Explode, Inspect or Exit inspect, Collapse and Put back. Materials read in Uzbek through a phrase table with a term-by-term fallback.
- Small Game views and windows: under 1440 × 810 pixels a compact layout (smaller scale, tighter margins, a narrower card, a seven-row tree, short hints). On every screen size the tree opens only from its tab or P, the card shows only while something is selected, and I or the eye button hides every panel but the hint. With a part selected at 708 × 440 the panels cover about 20–23 % of the view (28 % with the tree open, 2 % hidden); at 1080p about 11 %.
- H or `?` shows every key for the active mode, in the active language.
- The HUD publishes the free part of the view (`CarExplorerModels.HudSafeViewport`) for the explorer's placement and the inspect camera, keeps Remy's welcome toast clear of the panels, and reports when the pointer is over a control (`PointerOverHud`), so pressing the HUD never also clicks the car.
- XR: the same panels with the full layout stand on a world-space plate beside the car (1.06 m wide, 1.15 m from the head), pressed with the controller rays; it moves only when the visitor turns away or walks off.

### Inspect camera

- New on the desktop: F, the card's Inspect or a double click on a part flies the camera in 0.6 s to frame the selected node (else the deepest open level, else the taken-out assembly) in the free part of the view, from the visitor's side and clear of walls, furniture, Remy and other parts.
- Left drag orbits, right drag pans and the wheel zooms (down to a 0.02 m near plane); a click still selects and opens levels when the pointer moved less than 4 px.
- A new selection, or new bounds of the node after a move, re-frames it. A node that the current state does not draw cannot be inspected, and when the inspected node stops being drawn the camera flies back.
- F, Esc, Tab or a walking key flies back to the eye; the visitor's body never moves while inspecting. The state is shared through `CarExplorerInspect` with the HUD, the explorer and Remy. XR ignores it.

### Car internals

- New tool **Aurelle > Car Internals > Build** (`AurelleCarInternals.Batch`, `Assets/Editor/CarInternals`) builds the car's internals procedurally in a clean CAD style, measured against the car, and tags them with `CarPartInfo`: 1,025 parts in 38 assemblies of 9 systems, 283,268 triangles, 522 readable meshes in `Assets/Models/CarInternals` and 30 `CI_*` materials.
  - Suspension: double-wishbone front and multi-link rear corners with coilovers, anti-roll bars and subframes.
  - Steering: rack and pinion with electric assistance, tie rods and a column with two universal joints.
  - Brakes: pedal box, electromechanical booster and tandem master cylinder, ABS and ESC modulator, two diagonal circuits with braided hoses, electric parking-brake actuators.
  - Powertrain and driveline: clutch and flywheel, transaxle with final drive and differential, engine and transmission mounts, front and rear half shafts, an electric rear drive unit and a hybrid battery module.
  - Fuel: tank, lines and rail. Cooling: radiator module, coolant circuit, accessory belt drive and A/C circuit. Electrical: battery, power distribution, charging and starting, engine control unit and main harness.
  - Structure: sills and floor rails, the cross-car beam, seat frames, the roll structure and the rear crash structure.
- Each subsystem is one file under `Systems/`, found through an attribute, built with a shared mesh kit (`CarMeshKit.cs`) and palette (`CarPalette.cs`).
- An interference check raycasts every part against the car's body, cabin, wheels, V6 and floor and between assemblies; only declared contacts may touch. The current build reports "clear".
- The V6 is regrouped into 19 functional groups (crank train, block, heads, valve trains with camshafts, rockers and valves, intake with superchargers and filters, exhaust manifolds, timing, lubrication), tagged as the Powertrain's engine, with readable names and descriptions.
- Surface finishes: 14 tileable surfaces (sand-cast, machined, drawn, e-coat, zinc plating, forged, rubber, moulded plastic, stainless braid, embossed heat shield, heat-exchanger fins, corrugated loom, translucent reservoir, friction lining), synthesised in C# as 28 maps, 512 px on PC and 256 px ASTC 6×6 on Android. The meshes carry tangents and UVs in metres.
- Fine-detail parts (471 of the internals' parts, and the V6's 12 valve springs) are not drawn at Low.
- The exhaust, the front crash structure, the HVAC module and the timing-end engine mount clashed with the combined layout and are switched off.
- Half shafts replace the renderer of CarConcept's `Axles` bar.

### Performance

- The internals tool gives each of the V6's 182 renderers a merged one-submesh copy of its glTF mesh (37 copies in `Assets/Models/CarInternals/V6`), so the V6 draws 182 submeshes instead of 6,250 and SampleScene stores 182 V6 material slots instead of 6,250. **Aurelle > Car Internals > Restore V6 glTF Meshes** goes back.
- Measured with everything in place (Editor, Play Mode in batch mode, lab Mac): the normal view draws 107 submeshes and 213k triangles; X-ray or exploded 1,328 and 1.12 M at Ultra, 845 and 819k at Low; the main thread took 0.5–0.6 ms a frame in the latest run (1.9–3.5 ms in an earlier, busier one), with 136–368 B of allocations a frame.
- The HUD coalesces the model's changes to one diff a frame and pools the tree's rows (1,653 rows on 15 row objects).
- Render cost of the hero car's look and studio, against the build before them (1920 × 1080, render plus GPU sync, Editor medians): Ultra front 3/4 21.8 → 22.5 ms, wheel 18.9 → 20.3 ms, interior through the door 25.7 → 28.9 ms, the V6 alone 11.2 → 11.4 ms; Low 10.7 → 11.2, 9.5 → 9.4, 12.1 → 12.6 and 4.5 → 4.6 ms. The planar reflection costs about 1 ms at Ultra and nothing at Low; nothing at Low needed dropping.
- The hero reflection probe is 512 px; 1024 px would have grown Forward+'s probe atlas to 8192 px.

### Quest

- **Budget at Low** (`Rendering/AurelleQuestBudget.cs`, quality levels 0 and 1 only; nothing changes at Ultra): every second traffic car, pedestrian and cyclist is switched off before they start, the district's static boxes are merged per 40 m cell and material (3,138 renderers into 436 in SampleScene) and each premium car per material, small and flat pieces and the indoor cars, furniture and Remy stop casting real-time shadows, and cull-only LOD groups drop small district pieces, distant cars and people and the hero car's inner parts.
- **Low proxies of the hero car** (`CarExplorerLowProxy.cs`): at Low the car at rest is drawn from merged meshes, 27 draws in the normal view (95 renderers) and 61 in the X-ray overview (757); a proxy hides in the frame a part is selected, hovered, dimmed, moved or grabbed, so picking, highlight, take-out and the exact restore see the real parts.
- Measured in the Editor with a Quest 3-like stereo view at Low (`QPerfLab.RunLowXR`, lab branch), batches before → after: spawn 1,492 → 507, street with traffic 1,478 → 204, entrance from outside 1,681 → 492, the hero car from the front 1,158 → 384, the X-ray overview 1,958 → 429; the V6 out or apart and the exploded view stay at about 700–950 batches.
- The district's four 2K asphalt and concrete maps import at 1024 px on Android (the scene's estimated Android texture memory 71.1 → 64.0 MB).

### Glazed front and entrance doors

- The interior tool builds the glazed front (see Interior), and the desktop walkthrough hangs two hinged glass doors in its entrance frame.
- The doors open by themselves when the visitor's body or Remy comes within 2.5 m of the opening, on either side, swinging away from whoever opens them over 1.6 s, and close 3 s after the doorway is clear. E toggles them by hand within 4.5 m; a manual close holds until somebody next comes into the zone.
- One box collider per leaf covers it at every swing angle. In XR the opening has no doors.

### XR and desktop startup

- The scene chooses between desktop and XR before any other script starts. With an active headset or XR loader, the VR template rig is switched on at the exterior spawn and the desktop camera is switched off; once tracking settles, the headset is placed at the spawn facing the showroom. Previously the rig stayed inactive and the headset showed an untracked view.
- If an XR loader is running but no headset display starts within 6 s, the scene falls back to desktop mode.
- Without an XR rig in the scene, XR mode falls back to a head-tracked camera.
- In XR mode the Android XR hand-tracking permission is requested, and the rig's jump is disabled because A advances the explorer.
- Falling below y = −25 returns the player to the spawn, on desktop and in XR.
- Foveated rendering is requested at runtime; it takes effect only once the OpenXR Foveated Rendering feature is enabled (see the README).
- Android players (Quest) always start in XR mode and wait for the headset display; the 6 s desktop fallback now applies only to desktop platforms, so a slow headset start can no longer leave a Quest user in a desktop view without keyboard or mouse.

### Quest build

- **Aurelle > Build > Quest APK** and the batch method `AurelleBuild.BuildQuestApk` (`Assets/Editor/AurelleBuild.cs`) build SampleScene as a non-development IL2CPP ARM64 APK at `Builds/Aurelle-Quest.apk`. The build switches to Android if needed, sets IL2CPP, ARM64, ASTC textures and APK output, stops when the OpenXR loader is off or the OpenXR project validation for Android reports errors, writes a summary with the commit (`Builds/Aurelle-Quest.txt`) and the settings check (`Builds/Aurelle-Quest-check.txt`), and exits with code 1 on failure. **Aurelle > Build > Check Quest Settings** (`AurelleBuild.CheckQuestSettings`) only reports the settings and the validation.
- Project identity: product name Aurelle Showroom, company Aurelle and application ID `com.aurelle.showroom` (Android and Standalone) replace the template defaults; the version stays 1.0 (code 1).
- The Meta Quest target devices are Quest 2, Quest Pro, Quest 3 and Quest 3S; the original Quest is no longer listed in the manifest.
- Android builds leave out the Ultra quality level, which the Quest never uses: its pipeline (post-processing, SSAO, Forward+, HDR) no longer adds shader variants and build time to the APK.

### Input

- Arrow keys only walk; the explorer uses T, O, R, `[`, `]`, Page Up and Page Down.
- New keys: F inspects the selection (a double click on a part too), P opens and closes the STRUCTURE tree, I hides and shows the panels, H shows the help.
- A crosshair is shown while the cursor is locked, and clicks select at the screen centre. Tab frees the cursor for the HUD.
- Esc frees the cursor, clears the selection and closes the greeting; while inspecting it only takes the camera back, and the selection stays.
- The HUD's key hint and help match the active mode (desktop or XR), the state and the language.
- In XR, the trigger on either controller selects along that controller's ray, and the grip grabs parts.

### Collisions

- Walls, the three premium cars and floor-standing furniture get colliders at runtime in both modes, so neither the visitor nor Remy walks through them.
- The podium's colliders follow its shape instead of forming 3 m domes.
- The showroom floor gets a solid 1 m deep collider under its thin plane, so characters can no longer sink through it; if anything still pushes Remy below the floor, he is put back on it.
- The desktop eye height no longer drops when looking down.
- The entrance doors swing open and shut over 1.6 s instead of snapping (see Glazed front and entrance doors).

### Presenter (Remy)

- Remy starts his greeting whenever the visitor enters the showroom, from any side, and walks to a reachable spot 1.6–1.9 m in front of them instead of walking in place. His welcome shows as a toast on the HUD once he has arrived.
- He stays with the visitor while the welcome shows, leaves sooner once they start exploring, and stays at most 20 s after arriving.
- Afterwards he keeps a station beside the car the visitor attends (the hero car, or a premium display they look at for 3 s): never between them and the car, out of their sight lines to it, and not among or behind the parts they study (the taken-out assembly, the exploded car, or the node the inspect camera frames, once that camera holds still). He moves only when he has to, and after the greeting his routes go round the podium, never across it.
- He looks and points at the selected part's centre; head tilt and arm swing are corrected.

### Exterior agents

- Cars, cyclists and signals rebuild their runtime state when the scene loads, so cars no longer start their first lap the wrong way and signal lamps are correct from the first frame.
- Cars round corners, stop at stop lines, keep braking distance and yield to cyclists, pedestrians and crossing cars; a deadlock breaker resolves standoffs at junctions.
- Traffic car wheels turn: each moving car rigs its wheels at start from the model's geometry, with a steer and a spin pivot at each hub.
